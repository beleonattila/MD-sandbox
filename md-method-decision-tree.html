Choosing a method: the decision tree

Companion to the methods poster. The tree is read left to right — the first question is what you need to compute, not which tool you would like to learn. Most wasted simulation time comes from answering a Branch B question with a Branch C tool, or the reverse.

Check first: is the starting structure trustworthy?

MD amplifies a bad model rather than repairing it. Inspect pLDDT and PAE, protonation states at the relevant pH, missing loops and the oligomeric state before any of the branches below apply.

Check first: is the timescale reachable?

Estimate the barrier. At 300 K a 12 kcal/mol barrier is a millisecond event, which no unbiased trajectory will sample. If the answer is no, you are on the enhanced-sampling or rare-event branch whether you like it or not.

Check first: does an experiment already answer it?

HDX, NMR relaxation dispersion, room-temperature or time-resolved crystallography and cryo-EM heterogeneity analysis all measure ensembles directly. Simulation is most useful where it is restrained by one of these, not where it substitutes for one.

Mis-selections that survive peer review

Each of these is a category error rather than a technical mistake, which is why more compute does not fix any of them.

The claimWhy it does not followWhat would actually support it
"We ran 1 µs of unbiased MD and the pocket never opened, so it does not open."Absence of a transition in a finite trajectory is uninformative about a process slower than the trajectory. You measured your sampling, not the protein.An enhanced-sampling free-energy surface, or an ensemble model, or an explicit statement of the timescale you could have resolved.
"The generative ensemble model shows the closed state is 8%, so the opening rate is slow."Populations and rates are independent. A 92:8 split is compatible with microsecond or second interconversion — the barrier height is not encoded in the populations.An MSM, a weighted-ensemble calculation, or infrequent metadynamics. Anything with a time axis.
"The stability predictor gives −1.8 kcal/mol, so this variant will be more active."Stability and activity are different observables and frequently anticorrelated — active-site residues are often destabilising by design.Measure activity, or model the catalytic step. Use stability only as a filter against unfolding, not as a proxy for function.
"The docking score is −9.4 kcal/mol, so the binding free energy is −9.4 kcal/mol."Docking scores are ranking heuristics with no statistical-mechanical definition. The units are decorative.Alchemical FEP or absolute binding free energy, with a convergence analysis and, ideally, a measured congeneric series to calibrate against.
"We compared the minimised energies of the two conformers."That discards the basin volume, which is the entropic half of the free energy and often the deciding half.A free-energy difference: integrate the density over each basin, or run an alchemical or biased calculation.
"The MLFF gave a smooth trajectory, so the potential is fine."Machine-learned potentials extrapolate silently. Smoothness is not evidence of accuracy anywhere outside the training distribution.An uncertainty estimate along the trajectory, plus spot-check single points against the DFT level the model was trained on.
"AlphaFold gives two different models, so the protein has two states."Model diversity reflects MSA depth and sampling in the predictor, not a Boltzmann-weighted ensemble. There is no temperature in it.An ensemble method with a defined distribution — a generative ensemble model, or explicit MD.

Rough cost to a first defensible number

Order of magnitude, assuming a working structure in hand and one modern GPU or one CPU node. The dominant term is almost always human time on setup and validation, not the run itself.

RouteWall-clock to first resultDominant cost
Structure predictionMinutes to hoursNothing. This is the cheap branch and it should always be step one.
Generative ensemble modelHoursInference is cheap; interpreting whether the target is inside the training distribution is not.
Unbiased MD, single microsecondDaysSystem preparation, protonation, membrane building. Then the realisation that a microsecond was not enough.
Alchemical FEP, one perturbation edgeDaysSetup and mapping the perturbation correctly; convergence checking per edge.
Enhanced sampling, converged surfaceDays to weeksChoosing and validating the collective variable. Expect at least one restart.
Markov state modelWeeksAccumulating enough transition statistics, then defending the discretisation.
QM/MM free-energy surfaceMonthsCompute, plus defending functional, QM-region size and boundary treatment to reviewers.
