Molecular dynamics: a map of the methods

Every method on this poster is an approximation to the same object — the Boltzmann distribution over configurations, and the observables you get by averaging over it. They differ in how they compute the energy, how they sample, and, in the newest branch, whether they simulate at all.

Figures are order-of-magnitude. Throughput assumes one modern datacentre GPU or one CPU node; real numbers move by 10× with system size, cutoffs and hardware.

Parameter and dataset sizes are the published scale of representative models, not exact counts.

Compiled September 2026. The learned-surrogate half of this map is under a year old and will date fastest.

What all of it is approximating

Fix this first — the taxonomy below is just different attacks on these three lines.

The distribution

What a protein actually is at T > 0

p(x) = e−U(x)/kBT / Z,   Z = ∫ e−U(x)/kBT dx A crystal structure or an AlphaFold model is roughly the mode of p. States at 5%, 1%, 0.1% are not absent, only invisible.

The observable

What you actually measure

⟨A⟩ = ∫ A(x) p(x) dx ≈ (1/M) Σt A(xt) The ergodic swap of an ensemble average for a time average is the entire justification for running a trajectory. It only holds if you sampled long enough.

The gap that matters

Why function lives in the tails

kcat ∝ e−ΔG‡/kBT,   ΔG = −kBT ln(ZB/ZA) Rates depend on free-energy differences, so a 1.4 kcal/mol error is a 10× error in rate. This is why "1 kcal/mol" is the accuracy bar quoted everywhere below.

Layer 1 — three ways to get the energy and the forces

Classical, machine-learned, quantum. Everything else in the field sits downstream of this choice.

Classical force fields

Empirical potential, fixed bonding, fixed charges

U = Σb kb(b−b0)² + Σθ kθ(θ−θ0)² + Σφ Σn (Vn/2)[1+cos(nφ−γ)]
    + Σi<j { 4εij[(σ/r)12−(σ/r)6] + qiqj/4πε0rij } Integrated with velocity Verlet: mi r̈i = −∇iU. Long-range Coulomb by particle mesh Ewald.
Parameters
~10³–10⁴ fitted constants (atom types, bonded terms, LJ pairs, partial charges)
Fitted to
QM energies of small fragments + condensed-phase experiment (densities, ΔHvap, hydration free energies)
Data volume
~10³–10⁵ reference values; decades of hand curation
Data type
Scalar thermodynamic targets, torsion scans, dimer curves
Cost scaling
O(N log N) with PME
Timestep
2 fs with H-bond constraints; 4 fs with hydrogen mass repartitioning
Throughput
~10²–10³ ns/day for 10⁵ atoms, single GPU
Reach
10⁴–10⁷ atoms · ns to µs (ms on Anton-class hardware)
Tools
GROMACS, AMBER, OpenMM, NAMD, Desmond · ff19SB/OPC, CHARMM36m, OPLS4, OpenFF Sage, Espaloma

SuitsSolvation and membrane behaviour, complex stability, loop and side-chain flexibility, alchemical free energies, MD refinement of docked poses, cryptic pocket opening if you are lucky.

BottleneckTwo independent walls. Sampling: the transitions you care about are often 10³–10⁶× longer than what you can run. Accuracy: residual force-field error near 0.5–1 kcal/mol does not shrink with more sampling. No bond breaking, no electronic polarisation.

Machine-learned force fields

Regress the QM potential energy surface, then use it as a potential

E(x) = Σi Ei(hi),  hi = message passing over neighbours within rcut
E(Rx+t) = E(x),   F = −∂E/∂x  (equivariant by construction)
ℒ = λE‖E−Ê‖² + λF Σi‖Fi−F̂i‖² Forces come from autodiff of the predicted energy, so energy conservation is structural rather than fitted.
Parameters
10⁵–10⁷ (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, NequIP/Allegro) · 10⁸–10⁹ (foundation potentials: UMA, Orb)
Fitted to
DFT (usually ωB97M-D3 or PBE-class) energies and forces
Data volume
10⁵–10⁸ labelled configurations. SPICE ≈ 10⁶ drug-like molecules; open catalysis/molecular sets reach 10⁸
Data type
Coordinates → energy + per-atom force vectors (increasingly + charges, dipoles, stress)
Cost scaling
O(N) in atoms, but 10²–10³× classical MM per step
Throughput
~0.1–10 ns/day for 10³–10⁴ atoms; 10³–10⁶× cheaper than the DFT it replaces
Tools
MACE-OFF, MACE-polar-1, AIMNet2, Egret-1, FeNNix-Bio1, AceFF, Orb, UMA · ASE, OpenMM-ML, TorchMD-Net

SuitsReactive chemistry at near-QM accuracy, active-site energetics inside an ML/MM partition, small-molecule conformer energies and torsion profiles, systems where a classical force field has no parameters (metals, cofactors, non-canonical residues).

BottleneckOut-of-distribution silence — the model returns a confident number for configurations unlike anything it saw, with no built-in warning. Long-range electrostatics were absent until the 2025–26 polarisable and charge-aware models. Whole protein in explicit water, fully ML, is still not routine.

Quantum and QM/MM

Solve for the electrons; the only branch where chemistry can happen

ĤeΨ(r;R) = Ee(R)Ψ  (Born–Oppenheimer)
DFT: E[ρ] = Ts[ρ] + Eext[ρ] + EH[ρ] + Exc[ρ]
QM/MM: Ĥ = ĤQM + ĤMM + ĤQM/MM Empirical valence bond replaces the wavefunction with coupled diabatic states — far cheaper, and the framework in which electrostatic preorganisation was quantified.
Parameters
None for wavefunction methods; a hybrid or range-separated functional carries ~5–30 empirical constants
Fitted to
Nothing, or benchmark reaction/interaction energy sets for functional development
Data volume
Not applicable — this is the method that generates the data for the column to its left
Cost scaling
DFT O(N³) · MP2 O(N⁵) · CCSD(T) O(N⁷) in basis functions
Throughput
~1–10 ps/day of AIMD for 10²–10³ atoms on a full node. A converged QM/MM free-energy surface is a 10⁵–10⁶ core-hour project.
Reach
10¹–10³ QM atoms · fs to ps
Tools
CP2K, ORCA, Q-Chem, Gaussian, Psi4 · ONIOM and electrostatic embedding · Q (EVB)

SuitsBond making and breaking, transition-state geometry and ΔG‡, metal and radical centres, proton transfer, spectroscopic observables, generating training labels for MLFFs.

BottleneckCost forbids sampling. You obtain a few reaction paths, not an ensemble, so the number you report is conditioned on a starting structure you chose. Add functional dependence, QM-region size convergence and boundary artefacts — all of which reviewers will contest.

Layer 2 — turning forces into ensembles

Having a potential is not having a distribution. These are the three things you can do with one.

Unbiased forward integration

Let the physics run and hope it is ergodic

x(t+Δt) = x(t) + vΔt + (F/2m)Δt²
Langevin: mv̇ = F − γmv + √(2γm kBT) η(t)

SuitsLocal fluctuation, RMSF, contact lifetimes, water structure, anything whose relaxation time is shorter than your trajectory.

BottleneckExponential in barrier height. A 12 kcal/mol barrier at 300 K is ~10⁻³ s — unreachable. Also: convergence is not observable from a single run, so "it looked converged" is not evidence.

Enhanced sampling and free energy

Bias along a coordinate, then remove the bias

Metadynamics: V(s,t) = Σt'<t W exp[−(s−s(t'))²/2σ²]
TI: ΔG = ∫01 ⟨∂U/∂λ⟩λ dλ  ·  FEP: ΔG = −kBT ln⟨e−ΔU/kBT⟩

SuitsΔG of binding, ΔΔG of mutation, conformational free-energy surfaces, ligand unbinding paths. OPES has largely replaced well-tempered metadynamics as the default adaptive bias.

BottleneckThe collective variable. Bias the wrong coordinate and you gain nothing while appearing to converge. Learned CVs (Deep-TICA, VAMPnets, autoencoders) are the current answer and are themselves discriminative models — see below.

Kinetic model building

Many short runs → one kinetic object

p(t+τ) = T(τ)⊤p(t),   ti = −τ/ln λi Discretise, count transitions, diagonalise. The eigenvalues are relaxation timescales; the eigenvectors are the slow processes.

SuitsRates, pathways, state populations, mechanism. Recovers timescales longer than any individual trajectory. Also the machinery that reweights biased or short MD into Boltzmann-correct training data for generative models.

BottleneckNeeds enough transition statistics in the first place, and the discretisation is a modelling choice that changes the answer. Implied-timescale convergence is a diagnostic, not a proof.

Layer 3 — the machine learning split

Both halves are trained on simulation output. The difference is what they output: a number about a structure, or a structure.

Discriminative: five jobs

Objective: minimise E[ℒ(fθ(x), y)] over labelled pairs

Params
10⁵–10⁹ depending on the job
Data type
(structure or sequence, scalar or vector label)
Shared failure
Interpolates the training landscape. Learns correlation, not mechanism, so transfer to a new scaffold is unreliable.

Energy and force regression

x ∈ ℝ3N → E ∈ ℝ, F ∈ ℝ3N

The MLFF column of layer 1 is a discriminative model. It happens to be plugged into a physics integrator afterwards, which is what makes it feel like simulation.

MACE-OFF · AIMNet2 · NequIP · Allegro · UMA · Orb

Property and fitness prediction

sequence or structure → ΔΔG, Tm, Kd, activity

The workhorse of ML-guided directed evolution. Zero-shot from a protein language model, or supervised on a few hundred measured variants with uncertainty for acquisition.

  • Zero-shot: ESM log-odds, EVE, GEMME
  • Supervised low-N: ridge/GP on embeddings
  • Structure-aware: ThermoMPNN, stability oracles

Bottleneck: assay noise and epistasis. A model fit on one landscape rarely ranks a new one.

Collective variable and slow-mode learning

trajectory → s(x) maximising autocorrelation at lag τ

Finds the coordinate that enhanced sampling should bias. VAMPnets solve the variational problem for the transfer operator directly; TICA is its linear special case.

TICA · Deep-TICA · VAMPnets · SRVs · state-free reversible VAMPnets

Committor and transition classification

x → pB(x) ∈ [0,1]

The committor is the ideal reaction coordinate — the probability of reaching state B before A. Learning it directly sidesteps the CV-guessing problem entirely.

Neural committor networks · transition path sampling with learned committors

Uncertainty and out-of-distribution detection

x → σ(x), "should you trust this prediction"

The safety layer for everything above. Ensemble disagreement, Gaussian-process variance, or a learned probe on internal representations. Necessary because an MLFF fails silently.

Deep ensembles · GP/BNN posteriors · latent-space probes

Generative: six conditioning patterns

Objective: max E[log pθ(x)] or match a score / velocity field

Diffusion: dx = f(x,t)dt + g(t)dw, learn sθ ≈ ∇xlog pt(x)
Flow matching: dx/dt = vθ(x,t), ℒ = E‖vθ − ut‖²
Normalizing flow: log p(x) = log pZ(f(x)) + log|det ∂f/∂x| Only the flow gives a tractable likelihood, which is what lets a Boltzmann generator reweight to the exact target: w(x) = e−U(x)/kBT / pθ(x). Diffusion and flow-matching models are emulators of their training distribution, not of the Boltzmann distribution.
Params
10⁷–10⁸ typical (AlphaFold-derived trunks dominate)
Training data
Aggregated MD frames (BioEmu integrates >200 ms of simulation), the PDB/AFDB, mega-scale stability measurements
Data type
Coordinate sets, optionally with time index and MSM-derived equilibrium weights
Throughput
10³ independent structures per hour on one GPU — roughly 10⁵× the cost-per-sample advantage over explicit MD

1 · Forward simulation (learned propagator)

xt → xt+τ ~ p(xt+τ | xt)

Replace the integrator, not the force field. Learn the transition density at a lag τ far larger than a physical timestep, then roll it out. Keeps a time axis, so kinetics survive.

  • Trained on short MD at fixed lag
  • Error compounds over rollout — the central risk

Timewarp · implicit transfer operators · Score Dynamics · MDGen (forward mode)

2 · i.i.d. ensemble sampling

sequence → {x1 … xM} ~ p(x)

No time axis at all. Draw statistically independent structures directly from the learned equilibrium distribution. This is the branch that reports ~1 kcal/mol relative free energies against millisecond MD, and it captures cryptic pockets, local unfolding and domain rearrangement.

  • Gives populations, not rates
  • Generalisation beyond the training MD is the open question

BioEmu · AlphaFlow / ESMFlow · DiG · ConfDiff · BBFlow · DiffEnsemble

3 · Trajectory generation

condition c → (x1, …, xT) jointly

Generate the whole time series in one shot rather than stepping. Because the model sees the full trajectory as a single object, it can be conditioned anywhere along it — which is what makes modes 4–6 possible with the same weights.

  • Currently demonstrated on peptides, not large proteins
  • Fixed trajectory length is a real architectural constraint

MDGen · state-space trajectory models · latent-space dynamics emulators

4 · Interpolation (transition paths)

(xA, xB) → path between them

Both endpoints are clamped and the model fills the middle. This is the generative answer to transition path sampling: you know the open and closed states, you want the mechanism and the barrier region, and you do not want to guess a reaction coordinate first.

  • Paths are plausible, not necessarily Boltzmann-weighted
  • Needs a committor or reweighting step for a real rate

MDGen interpolation · Doob's h-transform samplers · generative TPS

5 · Upsampling

temporal: {x0, xk, x2k} → every frame
spatial: CG beads → all atoms

Two distinct problems with the same shape. Temporal upsampling recovers the frames you did not save. Spatial upsampling — backmapping — restores atomistic detail to a coarse-grained trajectory, which makes the CG/AA round trip usable rather than one-way.

  • Backmapping is the missing half of every MARTINI workflow
  • Recovered detail is a conditional sample, not the truth

MDGen upsampling · GenZProt · DiAMoNDBack · backmapping flows

6 · Inpainting (masked design)

xknown → xmasked | xknown

Freeze part of the structure, regenerate the rest. Structurally identical to motif scaffolding in de novo design, which is why the same diffusion machinery serves both. Applied to trajectories it becomes conditional dynamics: fix a binding-site geometry, ask what the rest of the protein does.

  • Loop and disordered-region modelling
  • Motif scaffolding, active-site retention, ncAA pocket completion
  • Open question: how far the conditioning propagates before it stops constraining anything

MDGen inpainting · RFdiffusion motif scaffolding · partial-diffusion conditioning

How to read the split
An MLFF and a diffusion ensemble model are both neural networks trained on MD, and they are commonly discussed together. They are opposites. The MLFF is discriminative and keeps the physics — you still integrate equations of motion, so you keep kinetics, transferability and the ability to add a ligand or a membrane. The ensemble model is generative and discards the physics — you gain 10⁵ in throughput and lose the time axis.
The honest ceiling
Ranking enzyme variants by activity requires ΔΔG‡ to roughly 1 kcal/mol. Alchemical FEP reaches that for stability and binding on good systems. Generative ensemble models now reach it for relative conformational free energies. Nothing on this poster reaches it for a catalytic barrier at throughput — that still requires QM/MM or EVB, one system at a time.
